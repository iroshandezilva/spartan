## Radius

<RadiusSamples />

<TokenTable prefix="radius" px />

| Role | Use | Utility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `radius.control` | Buttons, inputs, selects | `rounded-control` |
| `radius.surface` | Cards, dialogs, popovers | `rounded-surface` |
| `radius.pill` | Badges, tags, avatars | `rounded-pill` |

Containers are rounder than the controls inside them. The reverse reads as a
mistake: a sharp control inside a round card looks like it escaped. The
primitive scale also holds `none`, `sm`, and `xl`, which no role uses yet.

## Elevation

<ElevationSamples />

<TokenTable prefix="elevation" />

| Role | Paired with | Utility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `elevation.flat` | `border` on `surface` | `shadow-flat` |
| `elevation.surface` | `border.subtle` on `surface` | `shadow-surface` |
| `elevation.overlay` | `border` on `surface.elevated` | `shadow-overlay` |
| `elevation.modal` | `border` on `surface.elevated` | `shadow-modal` |

**Elevation is always paired with a border and a background, never used
alone.** In dark, a shadow against a dark surface is nearly invisible, so a
raised element that relies only on shadow disappears. In light, `surface` is
already pure white and `surface.elevated` cannot be lighter, so the shadow is
what does the separating. Each theme leans on a different cue, and using both
means neither has to work alone. Switch this page's theme and watch the
samples above: the shadow does the work in light, the fill does it in dark.

Shadow colour is the neutral ink at low alpha, not black. Black shadows grey
out the hue underneath them.

## Border width

<TokenTable prefix="border-width" />

`default` is every ordinary boundary. `emphasis` is a boundary that carries
state, such as a selected card or the invalid ring on a field, and it pairs
with `border.strong` or a status colour that meets 3:1. See
[Color](./color.mdx#borders-and-focus).

## Known gaps

- No inset shadow, and no elevation role for a pressed or sunken state. The
  released components express pressed state through colour and scale instead.
- The elevation samples are painted on the page background; the exact shadow
  read depends on what the consumer paints beneath a surface.
