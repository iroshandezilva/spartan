## Why OKLCH

Lightness in OKLCH is perceptual. A ramp built on evenly spaced OKLCH lightness
looks evenly spaced, which is not true of HSL, where two hues at the same
nominal lightness can differ by roughly 15:1 in measured luminance. Every rule
on this page depends on lightness meaning something.

## The scale

Seven families, thirteen steps each. Lightness at a step is **fixed across
families**, so `danger.700` and `primary.700` carry the same weight and a theme
can swap one family for another without re-deriving every contrast pairing.
Chroma peaks at step 500 and falls toward both ends, so a near-white step reads
as a tint rather than as a colour cast.

<ColorScale />

| Step | 0 | 50 | 100 | 200 | 300 | 400 | 500 | 600 | 700 | 800 | 900 | 950 | 1000 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lightness | 100% | 98% | 95% | 91% | 84% | 74% | 62% | 54% | 45% | 35% | 25% | 19% | 12% |

The light end is dense on purpose. Surfaces, hovers, subtle borders, and
selected backgrounds all live between steps 0 and 200 and need to be
distinguishable while all reading as nearly white. Measured on the rendered
ramps, the usable range is the same for every family: body text on a light
surface sits at **step 600 or darker**, body text on a dark surface at **step
500 or lighter**.

Primitives are shown here to explain the mapping. They are deliberately not
emitted as custom properties, so nothing above can be used in a component.

### Gamut

Colours that fall outside sRGB are brought in by **reducing chroma while
holding lightness and hue**, bisected to a fixed tolerance so the result is
deterministic. Lightness carries the contrast guarantee and hue carries the
family's identity; chroma is the only axis that can give way without breaking
a promise. Clipping RGB channels would shift both, so it is not used. The
`warning` family clamps at most steps, because sRGB has no dark, saturated
yellow; the policy resolves that predictably rather than hiding it.

## Roles

Every role is shown with its light and dark value from the published token
JSON, so the two themes sit side by side whatever theme this page is currently
in. The hex beside each swatch is derived from the same value.

### Background and surfaces

The whole system rests on surfaces separating from each other. In dark
especially, four near-blacks that measure almost the same are the fastest way
to make a selected row invisible.

<ColorRoles group="background" />
<ColorRoles group="surface" />

| Role | Reads as | Utility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `background` | The page | `bg-background` |
| `surface` | A card on the page | `bg-surface` |
| `surface.elevated` | A popover above the card | `bg-surface-elevated` |
| `surface.muted` | A recessed panel | `bg-surface-muted` |
| `surface.disabled` | An inert control | `bg-surface-disabled` |
| `surface.selected` | A chosen row, tinted **and** moved so it does not rely on colour alone | `bg-surface-selected` |

In dark, `surface.elevated` is a lighter step than `surface` because there is
headroom above it. In light, `surface` is already pure white and
`surface.elevated` is the **same value**: nothing is lighter than white, so
elevation in light is carried by shadow and border instead. Both facts are
asserted by test. See [Radius and elevation](./radius-and-elevation.mdx).

### Borders and focus

<ColorRoles group="border" />
<ColorRoles group="focus-ring" />

`border` and `border.subtle` are decorative and carry no minimum. `border.strong`
carries state, so it meets 3:1 against the page and a card. The focus ring is
checked against **three** surfaces in both themes: the page, a card, and a
raised surface. A ring that is visible on the page and vanishes inside a dialog
is the failure keyboard users actually hit. Utilities: `border-border`,
`border-border-strong`, `outline-focus-ring`.

### Actions

<ColorRoles group="primary" />
<ColorRoles group="secondary" />
<ColorRoles group="accent" />

Hover and active move **away** from the page in light and **toward** it in
dark, which is what keeps a pressed control looking pressed in both. Every
state is checked to differ from its resting state by at least 0.02 in OKLCH
lightness, and every filled role carries its own `foreground`, checked against
the fill at rest, on hover, and while active. Utilities: `bg-primary`,
`hover:bg-primary-hover`, `active:bg-primary-active`, `text-primary-foreground`.

`secondary` is a quiet button treatment, not a second brand colour. `accent`
is the emphasis colour for non-action highlights.

### Status

<ColorRoles group="success" />
<ColorRoles group="warning" />
<ColorRoles group="danger" />
<ColorRoles group="information" />

Each status family has three distinct jobs, and they are three roles because
they are three contrast requirements:

| Role | Job | Checked against | Utility |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `<family>` | A solid fill | Its own `foreground` | `bg-danger` |
| `<family>.foreground` | The label **on** the fill | The fill, including hover and active where they exist | `text-danger-foreground` |
| `<family>.text` | The colour used **as** text on the page | The page, a card, and the family's own `surface` | `text-danger-text` |
| `<family>.surface` | A soft tint, which is what a badge or a callout background is | Carries `<family>.text` | `bg-danger-surface` |

A status colour used as text is a different requirement from the same colour
used as a fill, and it is the one that gets skipped in systems that provide
only a fill. `Badge` is built entirely from `surface` and `text` pairs of one
family and never mixes families, so there is no combination a caller can
reach that has not been measured.

Mapping a product's own statuses onto these families is the product's job.
Spartant ships tones, not a status vocabulary.

## Light and dark

Light is the base. The dark theme restates only the roles that differ, so a
role added to light cannot be silently missing from dark: it inherits until
someone overrides it. The mapping never mentions a colour name; `color.primary`
aliases a step of the `primary` family in each theme, and nothing in the
semantic layer says "blue".

Body text sits at step 900 on a step-50 page in light, and at step 50 on a
step-1000 page in dark. Filled actions sit at 600 in light and 400 in dark,
which is the usable-range rule above applied in each direction. The tables on
this page are the authority for the current mapping; this paragraph describes
the pattern.

## Contrast policy

WCAG 2.2 AA, evaluated against **intended pairings** rather than every
possible combination. A role is only required to work where it is used.

| Use | Minimum | Applies to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Body text | 4.5:1 | Text below 24px, or below 18.66px bold |
| Large text | 3:1 | 24px and above, or 18.66px bold and above |
| Non-text | 3:1 | Focus rings, and any boundary that carries state |
| Disabled | Exempt | Measured for legibility, never blocking |

Disabled being exempt is WCAG's rule, not a shortcut, and it exists because a
disabled control that meets full contrast reads as enabled. Disabled state is
never carried by colour alone: the `disabled` attribute carries it for
assistive technology.

### When a pairing fails

In order of preference: change the mapping to a darker or lighter step, change
the family's seed if no step can serve the role, or record a reviewed exception
as data with the accepted ratio, the reason, and the approving issue. An
exception without an approving issue is not an exception; it is a failure that
someone edited around. There are currently no exceptions.

## Overriding a colour

Redefine the role's custom property, in both themes if the value should
differ. If you change `primary`, change `primary-hover`, `primary-active`,
`primary-foreground`, `primary-text`, `primary-surface`, and `focus-ring` with
it, and check the pairings above still pass; the package cannot audit an
override for you. See [Theming](../start/theming.mdx#overriding-a-role).

## Known gaps

- The pairing list on this page is a copy of the enforced list in the
  package's tooling, because that tooling is not a package export. If the two
  ever disagree, the tooling is right. The summary in the package's
  `THEMES.md` predates the status text roles and the switch pairings and
  quotes a smaller count than the enforced list.
- There is no exported API for computing contrast at runtime; the audit is
  build-time tooling. A consumer who overrides a role has to measure it
  themselves.
- The colour scale visualiser lives in a separate workspace app
  (`pnpm dev:color-tool`) and is not embedded here.
